# Hero Structure Specification — E01-002 Cave Mouth Occupation

Status: HERO SPECIFICATION / PRODUCTION DESIGN COMPLETE
Era: 01 — Lower Paleolithic / Early Human
Catalog position: 2 of 750
Structure ID: `cw:e01_cave_mouth_occupation`
Primary family: habitation / cave-threshold occupation
Default exclusion radius: 500 blocks
Compatible family exception: may co-generate inside an explicitly composed early-human cave-complex reservation when all linked members share one parent placement and deterministic layout contract.

## Purpose

Cave Mouth Occupation models repeated human use of the entrance and twilight zone of a natural cave. Its defining feature is not simply "people inside a cave"; it is deliberate occupation of the threshold where daylight, ventilation, weather protection, access to exterior resources, and limited interior shelter overlap.

The archetype must read as a cave entrance first, an organized occupation surface second, and never as a built dwelling inserted into a cavern. Compared with E01-001 Rock Overhang Camp, it penetrates into a naturally enclosed void and uses the mouth as a strong spatial boundary. Compared with E01-003 Deep Cave Refuge, its primary living and work activity remains within direct or near-direct connection to exterior light and airflow.

## Historical / Technological Context

This template represents Lower Paleolithic / early-human groups selecting cave entrances for temporary or recurrent occupation. Available technologies remain deliberately limited: percussion stone tools, fire, carried stone, opportunistic timber and brush, bone, hides where culturally valid, simple caches, and spatial behavior generated by repeated use.

Forbidden assumptions include formal masonry, shaped plank construction, doors, ceramic storage, agriculture, fixed furniture, permanent partitions, engineered chimneys, mine-like excavation, artificial lighting networks, ritual specialization that belongs to later cave-sanctuary archetypes, or deep subterranean settlement.

The hero value comes from spatial intelligence: smoke management, light-gradient use, circulation, dry-floor selection, hearth placement, work-zone positioning, refuse disposal, and selective use of alcoves.

## Footprint and Scale Classes

### S — Threshold Stop
- Entrance width: 6–14 blocks.
- Reservation footprint: 18–28 blocks wide, 16–28 deep.
- Occupied floor: 40–100 usable blocks.
- Interior penetration: normally 4–10 blocks beyond the mouth.
- Hearths: 0–1.
- Likely occupancy: 1–5 individuals.
- Use: brief shelter, weather refuge, hunting stop, temporary camp.

### M — Recurrent Entrance Camp
- Entrance width: 10–24 blocks.
- Reservation footprint: 28–46 blocks wide, 24–44 deep.
- Occupied floor: 100–280 usable blocks.
- Interior penetration: normally 6–18 blocks, constrained to twilight/ventilated zones.
- Hearths: 1–2.
- Likely occupancy: 5–12 individuals.
- Default hero scale.

### L — Multi-Zone Cave Mouth Camp
- Entrance width: 18–40 blocks or a clustered multi-mouth threshold.
- Reservation footprint: 42–70 blocks wide, 34–64 deep.
- Occupied floor: 260–650 usable blocks.
- Interior penetration: normally 10–28 blocks, but primary occupation must remain visibly tied to the mouth.
- Hearths: 2–4.
- Likely occupancy: 10–24 individuals across one or several occupation episodes.

Vertical entrance clearance should normally be 4–18 blocks. Large monumental caverns may exceed this only when human-use signals remain proportionate and do not falsely imply a later civic or ritual structure.

## Architectural / Behavioral Program

The structure uses a longitudinal gradient from exterior to interior rather than rooms.

1. **Exterior approach apron** — open ground connecting game trails, water access, or local terrain to the cave.
2. **Mouth threshold** — strongest light and weather boundary; the visual anchor of the archetype.
3. **Daylight work zone** — tool production, butchery, sorting, hide work, or other tasks requiring visibility.
4. **Primary hearth zone** — sheltered from weather but close enough to the mouth for smoke exhaust.
5. **Twilight rest zone** — quieter, darker, and more protected than the work zone while remaining connected to exterior circulation.
6. **Secondary alcove use** — optional cache, sleeping pocket, fuel storage, or short-term refuge.
7. **Interior stop-line** — a deliberate generation boundary beyond which normal habitation evidence drops sharply.
8. **Refuse/discard edge** — typically exterior, lateral to entrance traffic, or at a downslope cave-margin location.
9. **Water/drainage avoidance zone** — active occupation must not be placed in obvious runoff, drip pools, or flood channels.
10. **Smoke corridor** — not a built chimney, but a clear ventilation relationship between fire and entrance/cavern volume.

## Required Components

Hero admission requires:
- a naturally plausible cave entrance connected to a real or generated cave void;
- clear exterior-to-interior spatial transition;
- traversable protected floor inside the mouth;
- at least one human-use signal;
- for M/L variants, distinct daylight work and sheltered rest/hearth zones;
- evidence that hearth placement considers smoke escape;
- an interior stop-line preventing accidental transformation into a deep-cave settlement;
- no later-era manufactured materials or technologies;
- no destructive flattening of the surrounding cave geology.

Recommended probabilistic components:
- hearth or ash lens;
- lithic work scatter near natural light;
- bone or butchery traces;
- bedding analogue in a sheltered pocket;
- fuelwood cache;
- simple stone or organic wind deflection at one side of the mouth;
- wall-side resource cache;
- repeated-occupation hearth ghosts;
- exterior refuse concentration;
- animal-use traces in abandoned states.

## Procedural Generation Logic

### 1. Candidate cave-mouth search

Score candidate entrances for:
- entrance width and height;
- connected interior volume;
- usable dry-floor area;
- exterior approach accessibility;
- daylight penetration proxy;
- ventilation potential;
- roof stability and support;
- drainage/flood risk;
- slope and traversability;
- proximity to water and useful terrain;
- absence of conflicting structure reservations.

Reject poor sites rather than excavating a synthetic rectangular cave entrance to force the template.

### 2. Cave topology classification

Classify the entrance as one of:
- single mouth / shallow chamber;
- single mouth / branching interior;
- broad mouth / deep chamber;
- twin mouth / shared chamber;
- fissure entrance;
- collapsed entrance with residual shelter.

Classification influences layout but never removes the mouth-centered identity.

### 3. Mouth plane and light gradient

Derive a mouth plane from the exterior-facing cave opening. Generate deterministic distance fields for:
- full daylight;
- partial daylight;
- twilight;
- dark interior.

Primary work activity should favor full/partial daylight. Resting and sheltered hearth zones may occupy partial daylight/twilight. Dark-interior placement is strongly suppressed except for tiny caches or explicit condition hooks.

### 4. Occupation floor extraction

Find connected walkable surfaces from exterior apron through the cave mouth. Penalize steep drops, water, unstable rubble, low ceilings, and narrow choke points. Preserve natural unevenness; only minimal local smoothing around a hearth or repeated-use patch is permitted.

### 5. Ventilation-aware hearth placement

Hearth candidates require:
- safe distance from active runoff;
- sufficient overhead volume;
- direct or indirect smoke route toward the entrance;
- separation from primary sleeping pocket where scale allows;
- no placement in a dead-end low pocket likely to trap smoke.

The generator does not simulate fluid dynamics, but should use cave openness, mouth visibility, ceiling height, and path-to-exterior as ventilation proxies.

### 6. Behavioral zoning

Generate named deterministic subzones:
- `approach`
- `threshold`
- `daylight_work`
- `hearth`
- `rest`
- `cache`
- `refuse`
- `interior_stop`

For small variants some zones may merge; M/L variants should preserve readable differentiation.

### 7. Intervention pass

Human additions remain sparse and technology-valid. Constructed material should normally account for less than 20% of visible occupied surface. Never wall off the entrance as a later-period house.

### 8. Occupation-history pass

Repeated use may add:
- overlapping ash lenses;
- denser lithic concentrations;
- compacted pathways;
- old hearth scars;
- accumulated bone/refuse;
- rearranged local stones;
- partial sediment burial.

### 9. Integration pass

Blend all edits into existing cave and exterior terrain. Preserve cave continuations beyond the reserved occupation volume. Do not seal, erase, or overwrite third-party cave features or structures outside the negotiated generation volume.

## Biome / Environment Adaptations

### Temperate forest
- High fuelwood availability.
- More leaf/grass bedding and brush traces.
- Damp-floor and runoff penalties increased.
- Exterior work zone may merge into forest-edge activity patches.

### Boreal / cold
- Prefer deeper but still ventilated threshold positions.
- Larger hearth probability.
- Compact rest clustering.
- Stronger wind-screen probability at exposed mouths.

### Tundra / alpine
- Strong shelter-value weighting.
- Sparse timber and organic construction.
- Stone weighting for wind deflection.
- Snowdrift and exposure logic should preserve a usable entrance corridor.

### Savanna / dry grassland
- Shade and thermal buffering strongly increase candidate score.
- Work zones may extend farther outside the mouth.
- Water-distance weighting increases.

### Arid / desert
- Cave thermal refuge becomes a major driver.
- Sand/dust ingress affects abandoned variants.
- Organic traces are sparse.
- Dry wash / flash-flood channels are high-penalty placement zones.

### Tropical / humid
- Ventilation and drainage dominate site fitness.
- Organic traces decay quickly.
- Wet cave floors and standing water strongly suppress habitation zoning.

### Coastal
- Permit shell, marine bone, and shoreline-resource hooks when environmentally plausible.
- Saltwater or tidal cave mouths require flood/tide safety logic before occupation.

## Culture-Variant Hooks

Culture packs may modify:
- preferred cave orientation;
- group size and reuse frequency;
- hearth shape and placement bias;
- lithic raw-material preference;
- hunting/scavenging/fishing evidence weights;
- hide/bone processing intensity;
- sleeping arrangement;
- exterior vs interior work-zone weighting;
- cache behavior;
- pigment use only where era/culture rules explicitly allow it.

Culture hooks cannot introduce later-period architecture, formal cave temples, deep ritual galleries, permanent masonry, or unsupported claims tied to modern ethnic identities.

## Material Palette Logic

### Geology
At least 85% of visible cave structural mass should derive from local geology or compatible adjoining strata. Generated cave surfaces must preserve nearby material logic and avoid decorative palette swaps.

### Carried stone
Toolstone may differ from local geology to represent transport, but must appear as small-scale artifacts, nodules, hammerstones, or debris rather than structural masonry.

### Organic materials
Use locally plausible branches, brush, grasses, leaves, hides/furs where supported, bone, charcoal, ash, and food remains. Climate and condition state control preservation.

### Forbidden base materials
No planks, bricks, cut-stone masonry, glass, metal fixtures, ceramics, doors, chests, barrels, permanent torches, lanterns, redstone/powered systems, formal stairs/furniture, engineered ventilation, or constructed mine supports.

## Condition Variants

### Active / recently occupied
Fresh hearth, concentrated work debris, fuel reserve, readable circulation, low vegetation/sediment intrusion.

### Temporarily vacant
Cold hearth, intact activity zones, residual cache, light natural intrusion.

### Repeatedly occupied
Multiple hearth scars, dense lithic and bone signatures, compacted threshold path, overlapping use zones, rearranged stones.

### Abandoned
Organic decay, sediment ingress, partial vegetation at mouth, dispersed artifacts, possible animal takeover.

### Partially collapsed
Entrance or ceiling collapse narrows usable space while preserving a readable cave mouth. Collapse must be geologically supported and cannot create floating masses.

### Heavily silted / buried
Occupation floor is partly concealed by sediment. At least some visible or recoverable surface evidence must remain where the consuming platform expects readable archaeology.

### Animal-reoccupied
Human traces remain, but bedding/nesting or animal remains become dominant. This is a condition overlay, not a separate archetype.

### Later-era repurposed
A later culture may reuse the cave mouth through an explicit overlay relationship. Original early-human traces remain recoverable; later construction is additive and must not rewrite the base archetype definition.

## Loot / Occupancy Hooks

Loot represents occupation traces rather than stored treasure.

Potential finds:
- raw toolstone;
- simple stone-tool proxies where supported;
- flakes/debitage analogues;
- bone fragments;
- charcoal and ash;
- low-value food remains;
- hide/fiber fragments where supported;
- rare mineral/pigment pieces only through explicit culture hooks.

No chest is required or preferred. Finds should be distributed through cache pockets, floor scatter, work zones, hearth deposits, crevices, or archaeology-aware containers supplied by the target system.

Occupancy systems should cap active population to the usable threshold floor and ventilation model. Deep-cave crowding or permanent-village behavior is invalid.

## Gameplay / Readability Requirements

Without labels, the player should be able to infer:
1. the cave entrance provides deliberate shelter;
2. people favored the lighted/ventilated threshold rather than the deep cave;
3. work, fire, rest, and refuse behavior occupy different spatial relationships;
4. the group possessed extremely limited construction technology;
5. the cave existed first and occupation adapted to it;
6. the site may show one or several distinct occupation episodes.

## Additive / Non-Destructive Compatibility

- If reservation or protected-feature conflict cannot be resolved, reject or relocate the candidate.
- Compatible compound members must share one parent reservation and deterministic layout contract.
- Do not claim the deep connected cave system as exclusive space beyond the negotiated local generation volume.

## Validation Criteria

### Geometry
- Mouth is connected to plausible cave geology.
- Cave roof and walls are structurally supported.
- No floating rock masses or rectangular prefab cavern shell.
- Exterior-to-interior path is traversable.
- M/L occupation zones have adequate clearance.
- Water/runoff hazards do not occupy required living zones.

### Archetype distinction
- Primary activity remains mouth/twilight centered.
- Site is more enclosed than E01-001 Rock Overhang Camp.
- Site does not rely on deep-dark occupation characteristic of E01-003 Deep Cave Refuge.
- At least one daylight-dependent work zone exists for M/L scales.
- Interior stop-line suppresses accidental deep settlement.

### Historical / technological fitness
- No later technology in base state.
- No formal architecture dominates the cave.
- Hearth position is compatible with ventilation proxy.
- Spatial behavior reflects temporary/recurrent mobile occupation.

### Procedural fitness
- At least 100 deterministic seed samples per supported biome family before production admission.
- Test rejection rates as well as successful placements; unsuitable caves should be rejected rather than over-modified.
- Same seed/configuration must reproduce topology choice, scale, zoning, components, and condition decisions.
- S/M/L outputs remain recognizably the same archetype.

### Compatibility
- Verify 500-block exclusion against unrelated structures.
- Verify shared-parent family exemptions apply only to declared compatible members.
- Verify candidate rejection rather than destructive overwrite on protected conflicts.
- Verify the generator does not seize or rewrite long stretches of connected cave beyond its reserved volume.

### Visual hero bar
- Cave mouth silhouette remains natural and asymmetric.
- No obvious stamped rectangular chamber.
- Hearths and activity zones are not perfectly centered by default.
- Human traces form a coherent threshold-centered story from exterior and interior viewpoints.
- At least three materially distinct seed examples must pass expert review without reading as simple palette swaps.
- One example must demonstrate a difficult but valid entrance topology such as a fissure or twin-mouth chamber.
