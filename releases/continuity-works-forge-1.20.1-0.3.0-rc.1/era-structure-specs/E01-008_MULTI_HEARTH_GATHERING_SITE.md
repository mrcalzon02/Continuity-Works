# Hero Structure Specification — E01-008 Multi-Hearth Gathering Site

Status: HERO SPECIFICATION / PRODUCTION DESIGN COMPLETE
Era: 01 — Lower Paleolithic / Early Human
Catalog position: 8 of 750
Structure ID: `cw:e01_multi_hearth_gathering_site`
Primary family: camp infrastructure / coordinated group gathering landscape
Default exclusion radius: 500 blocks
Compatible family exception: may co-generate inside an explicitly composed early-human aggregation-camp reservation when linked hearths, shelters, processing areas, refuse zones, and circulation paths share one parent placement and deterministic layout contract.

## Purpose

Multi-Hearth Gathering Site represents a deliberately coordinated occupation landscape organized around several contemporaneous or sequential hearths. Its hero identity is not simply “more than one campfire.” It is the group-scale spatial order produced when multiple fire foci interact through heat, smoke, circulation, work specialization, social clustering, fuel demand, ash disposal, and repeated occupation history.

The archetype must read as a temporary aggregation site used by a larger group or several cooperating subgroups. It can support shared processing, cooking, warmth, social interaction, and short-duration coordinated occupation without becoming a permanent village, ceremonial plaza, formal courtyard, or later-period planned settlement.

Compared with E01-007 Hearth Circle, no single fire may completely dominate the site. At least two hearths must have distinct functional or social territories, and the site must exhibit meaningful inter-hearth organization.

## Historical / Technological Context

The technological ceiling remains Lower Paleolithic / early-human. The structure assumes controlled fire, simple hearth preparation, carried and local stone, fuel gathering, basic food processing, stone-tool work, bone/hide work where valid, bedding and temporary shelter support, and repeated occupation traces.

Permitted organization includes:
- several simultaneously active hearths;
- one primary hearth plus secondary task hearths;
- hearths assigned to household/subgroup clusters;
- sequential hearth relocation through time;
- shared central work or circulation areas;
- coordinated fuel gathering and ash disposal;
- spatial separation between smoky, hot, social, sleeping, and processing activities;
- integration with temporary shelters and natural shelter contexts.

The generator must not imply:
- masonry plazas;
- built courtyards;
- formal streets;
- permanent house plots;
- fixed civic space;
- ceremonial fire complexes unless supplied by a later distinct archetype;
- kilns, ovens, chimneys, grates, metal cookware, or industrial fire use;
- permanent storage architecture;
- livestock, agriculture, or village infrastructure.

## Archetype Boundary Contract

A valid Multi-Hearth Gathering Site must satisfy all of the following:

1. **Multiple meaningful hearths** — at least two hearths are functionally distinct, not decorative duplicates.
2. **Inter-hearth relationship** — spacing, smoke, circulation, or task allocation demonstrates that the hearths were planned or adapted in relation to each other.
3. **Group-scale occupation** — behavioral footprint exceeds the normal one-hearth household envelope.
4. **Shared circulation** — movement between hearth clusters remains legible and unobstructed.
5. **Smoke interaction management** — active hearths must not create implausible overlapping smoke zones without compensating spacing/orientation.
6. **Temporary character** — no permanent settlement fabric is required or implied.
7. **Subgroup differentiation** — at least two hearths must support different social, domestic, or work roles, even if subtle.

If multiple hearths exist but do not shape a shared gathering landscape, they remain separate E01-007 Hearth Circle instances rather than this archetype.

## Footprint and Scale Classes

### S — Dual-Hearth Gathering
- Hearth count: 2–3.
- Site footprint: approximately 18–30 blocks across.
- Typical inter-hearth spacing: 6–14 blocks.
- Occupancy: 8–18 individuals.
- Functional pattern: two household/subgroup fires or one domestic plus one task hearth.
- Shared circulation: one primary connecting corridor or open center.

### M — Clustered Multi-Hearth Camp
- Hearth count: 3–6.
- Site footprint: approximately 28–50 blocks across.
- Typical inter-hearth spacing: 8–18 blocks.
- Occupancy: 15–35 individuals.
- Default hero scale.
- Functional pattern: several subgroup hearths plus shared work/social zones.
- May include one older extinguished hearth generation.

### L — Aggregation Gathering Landscape
- Hearth count: 5–10 active/recent plus optional legacy hearth ghosts.
- Site footprint: approximately 45–80 blocks across.
- Typical inter-hearth spacing: 8–24 blocks depending on wind, terrain, and function.
- Occupancy: 30–70 individuals across one or multiple occupation episodes.
- Requires at least three distinct activity clusters and multiple circulation routes.
- Must remain temporary and discontinuous rather than forming permanent residential plots.

Large variants should gain organizational complexity rather than simply scaling all hearths outward from a perfect center.

## Architectural / Behavioral Program

The site is a network of fire-centered micro-territories joined by shared movement and work space.

1. **Primary hearth cluster** — one or more dominant domestic/social fires.
2. **Secondary hearth cluster** — subgroup, overflow, or specialist-use fires.
3. **Task hearth** — optional fire for butchery, hide treatment, hot-stone use, tool work, or food processing.
4. **Shared central circulation** — open space linking clusters without requiring a formal plaza.
5. **Subgroup rest sectors** — localized warmth/sleep/social zones around selected hearths.
6. **Shared work apron** — optional area between hearths for coordinated processing.
7. **Fuel staging zones** — distributed caches sized to total fire demand.
8. **Ash/charcoal discard margins** — kept outside high-traffic and sleeping zones.
9. **Smoke corridors** — interacting directional volumes derived from prevailing wind or shelter/cave airflow.
10. **Shelter interfaces** — optional brush, lean-to, hide windbreak, rock shelter, or cave-mouth relationships.
11. **Refuse edge** — group-scale discard area biased away from primary circulation and rest zones.
12. **Legacy hearth field** — cold hearth scars or ash lenses indicating prior episodes.
13. **Approach paths** — informal trampled routes from surrounding terrain into the gathering landscape.
14. **Quiet margin** — optional lower-traffic rest or recovery area, especially at L scale.

## Procedural Generation Logic

### 1. Parent-context classification

Classify the gathering landscape as:
- open plain/terrace camp;
- forest-edge clearing;
- river/lake terrace;
- rock-overhang aggregation;
- broad cave-mouth aggregation;
- mixed temporary-shelter compound;
- coastal resource aggregation;
- cold-climate wind-protected cluster.

Context controls wind, circulation, drainage, shelter integration, and resource logic.

### 2. Candidate macro-site scoring

Score candidate areas for:
- usable connected ground area;
- slope variability;
- drainage and flood risk;
- wind exposure;
- smoke-dispersal potential;
- fuel accessibility;
- nearby food/resource access;
- distance to water without occupying active flood channels;
- shelter opportunities;
- circulation permeability;
- existing reservations and protected features.

Reject sites that require broad flattening or destructive terrain clearing.

### 3. Gathering center derivation

Derive an irregular occupation centroid from terrain fitness rather than selecting the geometric center of the reservation. The centroid guides cluster placement but does not require radial symmetry.

### 4. Hearth-count and chronology selection

Select deterministic hearth count by scale and occupation history.

Assign each hearth one chronology state:
- `ACTIVE_CONTEMPORARY`
- `RECENT_CONTEMPORARY`
- `SHIFTED_SAME_EPISODE`
- `LEGACY_PRIOR_EPISODE`
- `ABANDONED_GHOST`

At least two hearths must belong to the current or recent functional landscape. Legacy hearths enrich history but cannot satisfy the archetype alone.

### 5. Cluster-role assignment

Assign hearth roles from a constrained pool:
- `DOMESTIC_SOCIAL`
- `COOKING_PROCESSING`
- `WORK_LIGHT`
- `WARMTH_REFUGE`
- `SUBGROUP_HEARTH`
- `HOT_STONE_TASK`

Role assignment influences spacing, nearby traces, and occupancy density. No role may imply later technology.

### 6. Wind and smoke field

Derive one macro prevailing wind vector plus local modifiers from terrain/shelter context.

For every active/recent hearth calculate:
- smoke corridor;
- ember/spark influence;
- likely comfortable upwind/crosswind sectors;
- conflict with neighboring hearth smoke;
- conflict with shelters and sleeping zones.

If two smoke corridors create persistent overlap across occupied zones, reposition, deactivate, or reclassify one hearth rather than accepting implausible layout.

### 7. Inter-hearth spacing solver

Minimum spacing depends on:
- hearth size;
- wind strength;
- simultaneous activity;
- local vegetation/fire risk;
- shelter material;
- expected occupancy;
- task specialization.

The solver should prefer irregular clusters and loose arcs over grids or perfect circles.

### 8. Shared-circulation graph

Create a graph connecting:
- approach points;
- hearth clusters;
- work apron;
- shelters;
- water/resource direction;
- refuse margin.

Paths should avoid crossing fire-focus safety bands and smoke-heavy sectors where possible.

For M/L sites require at least one alternate connection between major clusters.

### 9. Social/work territory generation

Around each hearth derive a local territory using weighted Voronoi or equivalent influence fields modified by terrain, wind, and role. Territories may overlap in shared work/social zones but should not create perfect partitions.

### 10. Shared-zone generation

Where several clusters overlap favor one or more:
- shared food-processing apron;
- group butchery area;
- toolstone distribution/work area;
- communal social space;
- fuel staging area;
- shared temporary shelter interface.

These zones remain informal and terrain-responsive.

### 11. Fuel-demand model

Estimate total fuel burden from:
- active hearth count;
- climate;
- occupancy;
- duration;
- fire role.

Distributed caches should be sufficient to communicate repeated use but must not become formal stockpiles or built sheds.

### 12. Refuse and ash management

Generate peripheral discard fields using downhill/downwind bias, traffic avoidance, and water protection. Separate hot ash handling from general bone/food refuse where the site scale supports it.

### 13. Temporal layering

A gathering site may represent:
- one intense aggregation episode;
- repeated seasonal reuse;
- shifting hearth positions over several episodes;
- contraction from a larger former camp;
- expansion from an earlier small gathering.

Temporal state should affect active-to-legacy hearth ratio and artifact density.

### 14. Terrain integration

Preserve natural microtopography. Human changes should mostly consist of cleared patches, compacted routes, moved loose stones, ash, fuel, bedding, and temporary shelters.

No formal roads, paved floors, plazas, walls, or permanent property boundaries.

### 15. Reservation and compatibility pass

Reserve the complete gathering-site footprint plus the Continuity Works minimum 500-block exclusion zone against unrelated independent structures.

Compatible child structures may coexist only when declared under the same parent reservation. Child compatibility does not transfer exemption rights to unrelated structures.

## Biome / Environment Adaptations

### Temperate forest
- Strong fuel availability.
- Higher fire-clearance requirements near leaf litter and brush.
- Cluster spacing may exploit natural clearings and tree breaks.
- Bedding and organic shelter traces are common in fresh states.

### Boreal / cold
- More hearths may be simultaneously active for warmth.
- Clusters become tighter but smoke-conflict rules remain strict.
- Larger fuel demand and caches.
- Windbreak/shelter family integration becomes more likely.

### Tundra / alpine
- Sparse fuel constrains total active hearth count.
- Strong wind causes directional clustering behind terrain protection.
- Stone-dominant fire containment and route markers.
- Occupation density favors compact lee zones.

### Savanna / dry grassland
- Wildfire risk increases clearance radii.
- Shade and water access influence gathering center.
- Hearth use may bias toward cooking rather than constant warmth.
- Fuel caches remain modest and dispersed.

### Arid / desert
- Hearth count may be lower for equivalent occupancy.
- Strong emphasis on evening/night use and thermal buffering.
- Wind/sand direction shapes cluster placement.
- Flash-flood channels are strict rejection zones.

### Tropical / humid
- Drainage and smoke dispersion dominate site fitness.
- Organic residue decays quickly.
- Hearths favor dry raised micro-sites.
- Persistent wet ground reduces valid simultaneous hearth count.

### Coastal
- Permit marine-resource processing, shell/bone refuse, and fish-related work hooks.
- Wind strength may significantly stretch cluster spacing.
- Tidal and storm-surge areas are invalid occupation surfaces.

## Material Palette Logic

### Hearth materials
Use local or plausibly carried unworked stone, bare mineral substrate, packed earth, ash, charcoal, and scorched surfaces.

### Fuel
Use locally plausible wood, brush, dung only where explicitly supported by the broader integration and era logic, or other period-valid combustible material. Fuel type must respond to biome availability.

### Activity residues
May include stone-tool debris, bone, food remains, hide/fiber traces, bedding material, heated-stone proxies, and ash/charcoal distributions.

### Temporary shelter materials
May include branches, brush, hides, grasses, stones, and other E01-004 to E01-006-valid materials when those families are composed into the parent site.

### Forbidden materials
No cut masonry, brick, ceramic hearths, metal, glass, planks as manufactured architecture, barrels, chests as default storage, formal furniture, powered components, permanent paving, fences, or engineered drainage.

## Condition Variants

### Active aggregation
Several lit/recent hearths, fresh fuel, dense occupation traces, open circulation, active work zones.

### Contracting gathering
One or two hearths remain active while others are recently extinguished; subgroup territories visibly shrink.

### Recently vacated
Cold hearths, intact fuel remnants, bedding/work traces, strong circulation paths, minimal regrowth.

### Repeated seasonal aggregation
Multiple overlapping hearth generations, compacted routes, strong refuse margins, old and new ash lenses, shifting cluster centers.

### Abandoned
Hearths cold and eroding, organic traces decayed, paths softening, vegetation intrusion beginning.

### Weather-damaged
Rain, flood-edge erosion, wind scatter, snow/frost disturbance, or sediment partially obscures hearth relationships without destroying the overall gathering pattern.

### Archaeological trace
Mostly hearth stains, charcoal, heat-affected stone, lithic/bone concentrations, compacted surfaces, and sediment relationships remain.

### Later repurposed
A later-period camp or structure may reuse the same favorable terrain through an explicit overlay relationship. Early-human hearth field evidence must remain recoverable and the new layer must be additive rather than replacing the base definition.

Required built infrastructure: none.

- fuel availability proportional to hearth count;
- water within practical reach but outside flood hazard;
- access to food/game/foraging resources;
- adequate smoke dispersion;

Formal roads, permanent utilities, agriculture, fencing, storage buildings, drainage systems, and settlement grids are invalid dependencies.

## Loot / Occupancy Hooks

Loot represents distributed occupation traces rather than treasure storage.

Potential finds:
- toolstone and flakes;
- simple stone-tool proxies;
- charcoal/ash;
- heated-stone fragments;
- bone and food remains;
- hide/fiber scraps where supported;
- fuel remnants;
- carried resource nodules;
- rare pigment/mineral pieces only through explicit culture hooks.

No chest is required or preferred. Distribution should follow hearth roles and shared zones rather than random uniform scatter.

Occupancy logic should:
- assign individuals/subgroups to hearth territories;
- keep active population within thermal, smoke, and circulation capacity;
- vary density through time;
- avoid crowding every hearth equally;
- allow shared work zones to draw temporary cross-cluster population.

## Gameplay / Readability Requirements

Without labels, a player should be able to infer:
1. several fires belong to one coordinated temporary occupation;
2. different hearths serve different people or tasks;
3. circulation and smoke influence where people gather;
4. some hearths may be older than others;
5. the site supported a larger temporary group than a single household hearth;
6. the landscape is organized but not urban, permanent, or formally planned.

## Validation Criteria

### Hearth-system validation
- At least two valid hearths meet E01-007 fire-safety and material rules.
- Active hearths have non-conflicting safety bands.
- Smoke corridors do not make primary occupied zones implausible.
- Hearth role differentiation is visible in geometry or residue pattern.

### Spatial validation
- Shared circulation connects major clusters.
- No required route crosses active flame/spark focus.
- M/L variants have alternate movement options.
- Work, sleep, fuel, refuse, and ash zones do not collapse into one uniform ring.

### Archetype validation
Reject if:
- one hearth dominates all activity and others are decorative;
- hearths behave as unrelated standalone placements;
- the layout becomes a permanent village plot system;
- a formal plaza/courtyard is implied;
- later-period fire technology appears.

### Historical/technological validation
- Materials remain within Lower Paleolithic capability.
- No masonry, metal, ceramics, built ovens, or permanent settlement infrastructure.
- Group organization is expressed behaviorally rather than architecturally.

### Procedural validation
Before production admission, test at least 100 deterministic seeds per supported major biome/context combination for:
- hearth overlap;
- smoke conflict;
- blocked circulation;
- invalid fire substrate;
- terrain destruction;
- reservation leakage;
- role duplication collapse;
- inaccessible clusters;
- invalid chronology state.

Same seed/configuration must reproduce materially equivalent hearth topology and chronology.

### Compatibility validation
- Verify minimum 500-block exclusion against unrelated structures.
- Verify explicit child-family exemptions work only inside the parent reservation.
- Verify third-party/protected structures cause rejection or relocation rather than overwrite.

### Visual hero bar
- No perfect ring of identical hearths.

- At least three materially distinct hero seed examples must survive expert review without reading as palette swaps.
